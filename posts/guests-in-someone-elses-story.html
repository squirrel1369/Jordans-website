Chaplain Chat • August

Guests in Someone Else’s Story

This week, I witnessed something at a patient's bedside that caught me off guard.

It wasn't inappropriate. It wasn't unsafe. It was simply just something I had never seen before.

For a brief moment, I tried to make sense of it. I wanted to compare it to what I had seen before, to quietly sort it into the categories of "normal" or "unusual."

Just when I think I've seen it all, I am gently reminded that I haven't.

We arrive at the very end of people's stories.

We may spend days, weeks, or months with a family, but we are stepping into relationships that have been built over decades. We don't know the private jokes they've shared, the arguments they've survived, the rituals they've created, or the countless ways they've learned to express love to one another. What may seem unfamiliar to us may be perfectly natural to them.

The ways people comfort one another, the words they choose, the silence they keep, the humor they use, the tenderness they show, and even the things that make us pause are often reflections of a lifetime together.

One of the greatest privileges—and challenges—of this work is recognizing that not everything needs interpretation. Sometimes our role isn't to understand every interaction. It's simply to witness it with curiosity instead of judgment and to create space for people to love one another in the ways they always have.

There is always another way someone grieves.
Another way someone says goodbye.
Another expression of love I haven't encountered yet.

Remain teachable, stay humble, and remember we are guests in someone else's story.

Thank you for the countless ways each of you creates space for patients and families to be fully themselves.

Have a weekend!
