Chaplain Chat • August

Holy Ground & An Eight-Foot Nutcracker

One of the ✨ privileges ✨ of this work is being invited into people’s spaces and their homes. We see a lot of wild things in people’s homes and rooms, and gardens and backyards. Things some might call trash. Decor that would never be found in a Southern Living catalog. Dishes and furniture not even made anymore.

This week I walked into a home with an eight-foot nutcracker — so tall it made the Christmas tree look like a shrub. And it got me thinking about what we really mean when we say we “meet people where they are.” Because sometimes, I’m not sure about you, but where they are is somewhere I don’t necessarily want to be. It can be really hard to meet people where they are when it’s not something we can fully grasp.

And yet the moment I walk in I want to hear about the knickknacks thrifted in moments of delight. I want to be walked through each room, hearing the stories behind the artifacts. I want to be offered a seat on the couch with the plastic covering — temporary, they’ll say, planning to replace it, just haven’t had the time. I want to notice the boxes still being unpacked, dust settling on their lids.

These spaces are layered with time, memory, and evidence of living. They may feel cluttered, unfamiliar, or uncomfortable — and they are also holy ground. They are places of trust. To be invited in is no small thing.

We don’t have to love every space we enter. But we do get the privilege of honoring what those spaces hold. Stories. Survival. Personality. Longing. Joy. Grief.

This week, I invite you to notice the environments you walk into. What might the space be telling you about the person who lives there? What story is being shared without words?

What a privilege it is to be welcomed into so many lovely homes.

I hope you all have a most wonderful weekend and find peace in whatever place you call home right now. Drink water, do your laundry, call your loved ones, and call if you need anything ✨
